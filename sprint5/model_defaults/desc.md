Add per-field control over model defaults in `polyfactory.defaults`, so a factory says which fields take their model's declared default. Helpers and summary fields that list fields give their names, in name order.

`declared(field_meta)` reports whether a field declares a default, telling one declaring nothing from one defaulting to `None`. `value_of(field_meta)` returns it, `None` where none is declared, and `is_none(field_meta)` reports a declared `None`.

`is_factory(field_meta)` says whether the model exposed a callable rather than a realised value: a dataclass resolves its `default_factory` first, pydantic hands the callable over. `called(value)` calls it, reading `None` from a raiser. `is_mutable(value)` reports a value two builds would share and `fresh(value)` copies a list, dict, set or bytearray, so `taken_from(field_meta)` gives what a build takes.

`names_declaring`, `names_lacking`, `names_by_factory` and `mutable_names` list a model's fields with a declared default, without one, with a factory one, and with a shared one; `defaults_of` maps each declared name to what `taken_from` gives.

`ALL` and `NONE` are `"all"` and `"none"` and `SELECTIONS` a tuple of both, which `is_selection(value)` recognises alone, not `True` or `False`. A selection is wholesale as `True`, `False` or either name, or per field as a collection of names or a predicate on one. `readable(selection)` is false for anything else, mappings included, and an unreadable one is never refused: it takes no default. `reaches(selection, field_name)` checks one name. `named_by` and `unknown_names` list what a selection spells out and what a model lacks; `verify(selection, field_metas)` raises `polyfactory.exceptions.MissingDefaultException` naming it.

`selected` and `skipped` split the fields, one declaring nothing never taken. `takes_any` and `takes_all` report the whole model, the latter holding where nothing declares one. `takes_none` names those taking a `None` default, `takes_factory` and `takes_value` split the taken by how declared, and `applied(selection, field_metas, given)` maps each taken field to its value, less what the caller gave. `fits(field_meta)` reports whether a declared default is of its field's annotated type, passing what it cannot check; `mismatched` names the rest.

`Default` and `Generate` are factory fields taking and generating a field against the selection. `wanted(marker)` answers `True` to take, `False` to generate, `None` for neither, and `resolve(selection, field_meta, marker)` returns `True` or `False` for one field, an explicit marker overriding the selection, and false where the field declares nothing. `check(field_meta, marker)` raises `MissingDefaultException`, naming the field, when `Default` is used without one.

`summarise(selection, field_metas)` returns a summary exposing `declaring`, `lacking`, `taken`, `generated`, the `by_factory` and `by_value` split, the `suspect` defaults `mismatched` names, the `values` `applied` gives, `takes(name)`, and booleans `narrowed` where anything is still generated, `wholesale` where `takes_all` holds, `untouched` where `takes_any` does not, and `sound` where nothing is suspect. `only(names)` and `excluding(names)` build predicates reaching the named fields and all but those.

A factory verifies its selection, and building and covering both honour `__use_defaults__`. A per-field selection, a collection or a predicate, does not reach a nested model, while a wholesale one does; `spells_out(selection)` is true for the former, false for the latter, so `propagated(selection)` gives `NONE` for one and the selection for the other.
